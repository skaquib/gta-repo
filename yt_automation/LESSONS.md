# FramedFacts lessons: our mistakes and what the winners do

Owner, 2026-11-05: "Learn from other and your mistakes." Read this before planning, making or uploading
anything. Every rule below comes from something that actually went wrong, or from measured data.

## Our mistakes (Oct-Nov 2026) and the rule each one created

| # | What went wrong | What it cost | Rule from now on |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Image generation silently fell back to the owner's paid AI Studio key when the free route hit a rate limit. | ~Rs 478 of the owner's Rs 500 | No paid key ever sits in the environment. One free route only. Every job is booked in `free_credit_ledger.json` before it runs, under a hard cap. The ledger is locked and fails safe (a read error stops the job, never resets to zero). |
| 2 | We built a 10-minute long video for a channel whose long videos had never been shown in Browse, Suggested or Search (the cat long video: 0 views from any of them). | ~Rs 5,600 of free credit, two days of work, 11 views | Before choosing a format, read the channel's traffic sources for that format. **Shorts first** until a Short series regularly passes 10K views; only then a long video, linked from those Shorts. |
| 3 | SEO was presented as if it would bring reach. | The owner's trust | SEO helps slowly, months later. It is never the plan for reach on a small channel. Reach comes from the Shorts feed: first second, retention, loops, a format people come back for. |
| 4 | The expensive version came first (a 10-minute realistic AI video) instead of the cheapest test of the idea. | Same as 2 | Test every idea in its cheapest form first: a Rs 0 Short (Blender, local voice), or Shorts cut from assets we already paid for. |
| 5 | Niche hopping: 120 sports Shorts, then eggs, pets, the deep ocean. | Each switch resets who the Shorts feed shows us to | One lane and one format for at least 15-20 uploads before judging it. |
| 6 | The deep-ocean Short opened on a dark close-up that doesn't read on a phone. It stalled at ~170 views; the pet and egg Shorts got ~1,250 on day 1. | One test wasted | The first frame is bright, high-contrast, readable at thumbnail size, and moving in the first second. Stakes in the first words. |
| 7 | The first cut of the food-critic Short ran 45 s. | Would have hurt retention | Shorts run **25-35 s** (the winners' median is 24-35 s). Cut the weakest beats, not the jokes' timing. |
| 8 | The first deep-ocean render was slow zooms on still images; the owner rejected it. | A full render | Real motion in every shot. QA runs a frozen-picture check (nothing may hold still for 1 s). |
| 9 | AI footage put fish and a shark below 8 km (after the line "fish can't live this deep"), ice at 1-11 km, and turned whales into humpbacks. | Re-generations, near-misses | Check every shot against the place and time the narration is at that moment. "Empty" scenes get "no animals" in both prompts. Trim clips before anatomy drifts. |
| 10 | The cloud voice read 186 words a minute; the long video fell to 7:56. | A re-render | Long videos 150-165 wpm and 8:00+. Shorts can be quicker but keep a beat before each punchline. |
| 11 | Upload settings were trusted, not checked: the slot picker scheduled 6:00 PM instead of the owner's 6:30 PM, and the AI-content label was sent under a field name YouTube ignores. | Caught just in time | After every upload, read back privacy, publish time, category, captions and labels from the API and fix anything that differs. |
| 12 | Promising results before data existed. | Trust | Report numbers with a comparison, and say "too early" plainly when it is. |
| 13 | Long chains of fixes after a full render. | Hours of render time | Look-test stills of every shot before the full render (done right for the food-critic Short: 5 problems fixed in stills). |
| 14 | The food-critic Short was built start to finish (idea, script, look, 5 h render) without showing the owner the idea or a sample first. The owner rejected it: "I don't ask for this, it is not good." | A day of work | **The owner approves in steps:** the idea in one or two lines, then one sample frame, then the script. Nothing renders before all three are approved. |
| 15 | Captions on our Shorts sat at y 1600 of 1920, under the text YouTube lays over a Short on a phone (channel name, title, roughly y > 1550). End questions were open ("Which animal should complain next?"). | Sound-off viewers lose the line; open questions get few answers | **Captions and calls to action stay above y 1500.** End on a two-way pick (one word to type, e.g. "Sloth or koala?"), seed it as the first comment for the owner to pin, and give a reason to like answers ("the most-liked answer gets the next video") -- then keep that promise. |
| 16 | The panda render's watcher hit its 2-hour limit and was not restarted. About 5 minutes later the cloud machine was reclaimed as idle and the render died at frame 256 of 757. | ~10 min lost, a missed promised time | **During any long render, a background watcher must always be running.** When one hits its time limit, start a new one at once. Detach the render itself (nohup setsid) so the watcher can stop without killing it; resume skips frames already made. |
| 17 | 143 Shorts, none past 2,737 views; every one stopped at YouTube's first test batch (~1,000-1,500). We judged videos by look, facts and % viewed, while median "stayed to watch" was 35% and shares were 10 in 76K views. Topics jumped from sports to eggs, pets, ocean and panda. | Seven months, no growth (CHANNEL_DIAGNOSIS.md) | **Stayed-to-watch decides reach.** Frame 1 is the payoff in motion; check stayed-to-watch at 24-48 h and drop any opening style under 50%. One lane, test hooks inside it. |
| 18 | Our 8 latest Shorts, first 2 s side by side (owner: "the hook is the first second"): every one opens on a still poster -- headline on top, caption below, subject in the middle -- and nothing changes from 0.0 s to 2.0 s. Those who stay are still there at 3 s (97%), so the 40-80% who swipe decide in the first second. Our best (egg in a snake, 58% stayed) simply had the strangest image. | The swipe | **Frame 1 is an event, not a poster.** Something already happening at 0.0-0.5 s (a fall, a bite, a crack, a collision); open on the strangest image of the whole video; at most one 2-4 word text, no caption sentence, borders or "full video" button in the first second; sound from 0.0 s. In the feed there is no thumbnail -- frame 1 is the thumbnail. Title: under 50 characters, a tension the first frame doesn't already answer, one emoji. The owner approves the first-second sheet (`first_second_sheet.py`), not one still. |
| 19 | Penny Short (Dummy Test 1): a "wind" noise bed ran under the first 8 s of narration only ~5 dB below the voice in the speech band (louder than it in places), and its one-pole filter left it a full-band hiss. The owner: "noise, the main voice is not hearable". Our QA measured loudness, black and frozen frames, never whether the voice was audible over the bed. Caught before release; the quicksand mix had the same problem. | Hook audio | **The narrator is always on top.** Any sound under narration stays >= 12 dB below the voice in the speech band (300-3400 Hz) for every second the narrator speaks: `dummy_shorts.py clarity NAME` (runs after every compose; a dip under 12 dB lasting 1 s or more fails). Noise-based effects are filtered steeply (an air rush, not a hiss) and kept short; a big sound goes in a pause, not under a word. |
| 20 | Dummy Tests, stayed to watch (owner's Studio screenshots, Nov 7-8): penny 48.2% (wide shot, dummy a dot far below), hail 55.4% (impact on him, close), quicksand 55.2% (dummy big, already stuck). All three still stopped at ~1,300-1,400 views. Hail's retention curve holds 100%+ to 3.6 s, then loses ~45 points between 4 and 9 s: exactly the "Eight inches across. Almost two pounds. South Dakota, 2010." stats line, then "Let's start small" (stepping down from the record to pea hail). The end card loses another ~20 points. | The push to more viewers | **Big, close, in danger beats wide** (+7 points); the wide-shot opening is dropped. **The 5 seconds after the hook escalate, they never recite:** no numbers, dates or places read out right after the hook (put them on screen while the action keeps going), never step down from the hook to a smaller case, each beat bigger than the last. End by looping back to frame 1 instead of a 3-s end card. Aim: 70% stayed. |
| 21 | Sinking-car Short (Dummy Test 5): 694 frames took over 7 h of rendering on this machine, after a day of re-staging. The script was written first and the dummy then had to act it (tap a phone, press a window switch, lift a hammer); a stiff mannequin can't act, so the picture did not show what the voice said. The topic was a safety tip people have seen many times. The owner: "so much time is wasting, the script and the visual don't even match, the story is too much common." Scan of the same day (notes/trends/2026-11-10_breakout_channels.md): channels 2-6 weeks old get 40K-380K median views with impossible what-if premises, one title pattern and 1-2 uploads a day; "crash test dummy" had 0 small-channel breakouts. | A day and a half for one test nobody wanted | **Picture first, then words.** Only stories where physics does the acting (he falls, melts, is crushed, floats); never one that needs fine hand or face acting. **An impossible premise, not a tip.** **One title pattern** for the whole series. **Render budget: 2 hours per Short**, measured on a sample before the full render; over budget means simplify the scene or drop the idea. |
| 22 | "10 Seconds" series, Venus and ocean floor (Nov 11): on "hot enough to melt lead" the caption sat on top of the lead bar, and on "shrinks like a cup" it sat on top of the cup; a meter also crossed the crushed drum. The props stood on the ground, and the ground is where the caption (y 1380-1600) and the meters (y 1090-1290) are drawn. My look-test stills had no text on them, so I only saw it in the finished Venus video. | 99 Venus frames re-rendered; the ocean render restarted after 88 frames | **Look tests carry the real text.** `dummy_shorts.py preview NAME F1 F2 ...` draws the hook, label, meters, clock and caption on small stills; run it on every shot before the full render. Whatever the line names sits in the clear band (y 280-1060): lift the prop (a block, a pedestal) or raise the camera, don't hope. |

## What the winners in our lane do (measured 2026-11-05, their last 50 Shorts each)

| Channel | Subs | Uploads | Median length | Median views | Their one format |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Zack D. Films | 29.6M | ~35 a week | 33 s | 7.3M | 3D "How / Why ___" explainers, emoji at the end of the title |
| Nick Wilkins | 9.3M | ~17 a week | 27 s | 3.2M | Comedy sketches, "😂" titles |
| Pawesome Ranks | 1.0M | ~6 a week | 24 s | 1.8M | "Ranking ___ Moments 😂" |
| Events Untold | 56K | ~32 a week | 7 s | 14K | Very short animal clips |

What they share:
1. **One repeatable format.** The viewer knows what they get; the feed knows whom to show it to.
2. **22-38 s.** Short enough to rewatch; many end where they began (a loop).
3. **Volume.** 6-35 Shorts a week. Every Short is a new test in the feed.
4. **Short titles: a curiosity line and one emoji at the end.** No hashtag clutter in the title.
5. **Ranking and rating formats explode** (Pawesome Ranks reached 1M subscribers on essentially one format).
   Our food critic's star ratings are a rating format, so it can become our series: "A Food Critic Rates ___ 🤢".

## Checklist before every video
- [ ] POSTING HOLD (owner, 2026-11-19): while yt_automation/POSTING_HOLD exists, make videos and daily topics but upload NOTHING. The uploader refuses. Only the owner lifts it.
- [ ] ENGLISH + HINDI (owner, 2026-11-19): every Short is made twice from the same pictures: English (`am_michael`) and Hindi (`hm_omega`, Devanagari captions and labels, Noto Sans Devanagari font, no ≈ or · symbols). The Hindi one gets its own Hindi title and thumbnail text. India is our #1 country.
- [ ] FIRST WORDS = FIRST PICTURE = THUMBNAIL (owner, 2026-11-19): from 0.0 s the voice speaks the hook (5-7 words), the same words are on screen, and the picture shows exactly that thing, matching the thumbnail. No random opening clip, no silence, no build-up. The owner approves the opening line + first frame with the title and thumbnail.
- [ ] FREE CREDIT = VISUALS ONLY (owner, 2026-11-19): Google free credit pays only for pictures and video clips (anime, Veo). Voices use the free local Kokoro voices (narrator `am_michael` / `bm_george`, Jems and Kiro `am_puck`); scripts are written by us. `genai_clients.cloud_tts` refuses new Google voice lines.
- [ ] TITLE + THUMBNAIL FIRST (owner, 2026-11-19: "work on title and thumbnail most"): before any rendering, write 3+ title options and 2-3 thumbnail options and pick with the owner. Title: the key hook in the first ~40 characters (mobile cuts the rest), curiosity or danger, plain words, true. Thumbnail: one huge emotion or object, 2-4 big words, high contrast, readable at phone size. The title and thumbnail must ADD to each other, never repeat the same words.
- [ ] FIRST 3 SECONDS (owner, 2026-11-18, "very important", Shorts AND long videos): open on the most exciting moment from the END of the video (flash-forward), then a line that makes viewers need the answer (surprise, danger, something new to them). Never open on a slow setup or a title card. Long videos also need a click-worthy thumbnail (one huge emotion, 2-4 big words, high contrast) shown to the owner with the script.
- [ ] Idea checked with `topic_validate.py` (a version has already broken out somewhere).
- [ ] Cheapest form first. Rs 0 if possible; never paid credit.
- [ ] Same lane and format as the last uploads (rule 5).
- [ ] First-second sheet (`first_second_sheet.py`): 0.0 s and 0.5 s clearly different, strangest image first, no poster/caption/borders in second one, sound from 0.0 s (rule 18).
- [ ] Shorts 25-35 s; a beat before each punchline; the ending loops or asks a two-way question.
- [ ] Ending asks for a comment AND a subscribe, with a reason ("subscribe for test #N+1"), inside the last line, not a long end card (`END_LINE`, `END_COMMENT` in dummy_shorts.py).
- [ ] Seconds 3-9 escalate: no stats/dates read out after the hook, no stepping down to a smaller case; every beat bigger than the last (rule 20).
- [ ] Captions and calls to action above y 1500 (YouTube's own text covers the bottom; rule 15).
- [ ] Every fact from 2+ sources; every shot matches what the narration says at that moment.
- [ ] Look-test stills before the full render, with the real text on them (`dummy_shorts.py preview`, rule 22): the thing each line names is in clear view, not under the caption or a meter. QA: no frozen or black frames, loudness ~-14 LUFS for Shorts.
- [ ] Voice on top: narration >= 12 dB above music + effects in the speech band whenever it speaks (`clarity`, rule 19).
- [ ] Owner approved the idea, a sample frame and the script BEFORE making it (rule 14).
- [ ] Owner approves before upload. After upload, read every setting back from the API. Reads can lag for a minute after upload (the panda Short showed 0 tags, then 11): re-read before "fixing" anything.
